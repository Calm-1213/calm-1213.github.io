Project 01 / Soft robotics

Test Rig for Soft Fluidic Actuators

A low-cost, reusable platform for measuring peak blocked force. The challenge was making a simple rig produce useful measurements from two different actuator structures.

01 / The challenge

A consistent test, using accessible parts.

Soft actuators can visibly bend, but that alone does not show the force they can apply. I set out to build an adjustable platform that could hold different actuator geometries and measure their peak force against a fixed contact point.

The project focused on the test rig, rather than optimising or ranking actuator designs. A PneuNet sample and a fibre-reinforced sample were used to demonstrate the platform.

02 / The build

Build. Find the weak link. Revise.

Mechanical platform

Adjustable, simple fixtures.

I built the rig on a 400 × 150 mm wooden base. A toggle clamp held the actuator, while multiple mounting holes allowed the sensor support to be repositioned for different sample lengths.

Pneumatic revision

Fewer connections, fewer leaks.

The initial pressure-gauge layout leaked at its fittings. I shortened the air path and used a syringe, three-way valve and direct adapter connection for manual inflation.

Sensing revision

A practical measurement route.

The initial load-cell, INA125P and Arduino arrangement gave unstable readings. I replaced it with a commercial 5 kg electronic scale module and checked its reading with a known 60 g mass.

Test method

Keep the procedure explicit.

I zeroed the scale, aligned the actuator, injected a selected air volume and recorded the display on video. The maximum displayed mass was converted to force in newtons.

Silent 12-second lab demonstration: manual syringe inflation and the electronic scale display. No spoken explanation is required; the test method is described above.

03 / The evidence

One platform, two force–volume datasets.

Each actuator was tested at six injected-air volumes, with three repeats per level and five repeats at the highest tested volume. Error bars show one standard deviation of the repeated readings.

Source: Yuzhou Li, Test rig for soft fluidic actuators, Tables 4–5. Different sample geometries and inflation ranges mean these are demonstrations of the rig, not a controlled ranking of actuator performance.

What the results show.

The rig recorded increasing force with larger air input across most test points. The highest mean peak force was 0.991 N for the PneuNet sample at 35 mL. Greater variation at the upper test volumes highlights the effect of manual operation and contact alignment.

04 / What I learned

A useful measurement needs clear limits.

This was a manually inflated prototype without direct pressure measurement. Its outputs are force–volume results, not force–pressure characterisation. The 60 g check was a basic verification, not a full calibration.

For me, the main engineering lesson was to investigate instability, simplify the system and state exactly what the revised method could support. Future improvements would add pressure sensing, a more rigid frame and continuous force logging.

Actuator fabrication methods were informed by the Soft Robotics Toolkit. See sources and acknowledgements.
